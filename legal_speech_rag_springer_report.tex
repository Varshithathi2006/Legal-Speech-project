\begin{abstract}
Long-form multi-speaker judicial proceedings present significant challenges for automated information retrieval due to unscripted dialogue, acoustic variability, overlapping speech, and strict statutory citation dependencies. In this work, we present an end-to-end framework for legal speech dataset curation and grounded Spoken Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Spoken RAG). We curate a high-quality speech dataset comprising 84.98 hours of transcribed multi-speaker legal audio across 179 video proceedings (100 Moot Court competitions and 79 Supreme Court live-streamed hearings), generating 663,801 spoken words across 4,260 clean, indexed vector chunks. Our speech conditioning pipeline integrates FFmpeg 16\,kHz 16-bit mono PCM conversion, PyAnnote 3.1 neural speaker diarization for boundary drift mitigation, and Faster-Whisper \texttt{small int8} ASR with checkpoint-resilient \texttt{.partial.json} state preservation. For downstream question answering, we construct a 1024-dimensional BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5 vector store using ChromaDB HNSW indexing. We enforce a 21-rule hierarchical legal retrieval engine featuring canonical Act mapping and exact metadata section boosting (0.9500 similarity priority), eliminating cross-act section collisions. Grounded multi-tier LLM synthesis generates precise statutory answers paired with an interactive layman-friendly explanation generator and neural Indian English speech output (\texttt{edge-tts}). Empirical evaluation demonstrates 100.0\% Context Precision (@0.35 threshold), 0\% cross-act contamination, and an end-to-end latency below 12.28 seconds.

\keywords{Speech Processing \and Speaker Diarization \and Spoken RAG \and Legal Question Answering \and Vector Embeddings \and Neural Speech Synthesis}
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
Legal proceedings generate immense volumes of unstructured multi-speaker audio recordings, ranging from 1 to 3 hours per session. Extracting actionable legal insights, specific statutory interpretations, or counsel arguments from these long-form recordings is computationally complex and labor-intensive. Traditional Keyword Spotting (KWS) and unsegmented Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) fail to maintain speaker provenance, frequently suffering from word error rate spikes in unscripted Indian courtroom environments.

Furthermore, applying standard dense vector Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to legal corpora introduces severe \textit{vector space collisions}. Generic statutory section numbers (e.g., ``Section 9A'' of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 vs. ``Section 9'' of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996) share near-identical embedding trajectories in dense semantic space, leading to cross-act contamination and downstream Large Language Model (LLM) hallucinations.

To resolve these dual challenges, this paper makes the following key contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Curated Legal Speech Dataset}: A benchmark dataset consisting of 84.98 transcribed audio hours, 663,801 spoken words, and 4,260 indexed vector chunks derived from 100 Moot Court proceedings and 79 Supreme Court Constitution Bench live-streams.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint-Resilient Audio Pipeline}: A 4-stage pipeline combining PyAnnote 3.1 neural diarization, Faster-Whisper \texttt{small int8} ASR with segment-level fault recovery, and dialogue sliding-window chunking (150--300 words).
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Metadata-Boosted Vector RAG}: A ChromaDB vector database leveraging 1024-dim BGE-Large embeddings with canonical Act mapping, exact section ID boosting ($0.9500$ rank priority), and 21 hierarchical routing rules.
    \item \textbf{Spoken Answer \& Plain-English Synthesis}: Grounded answer synthesis coupled with neural Indian English text-to-speech (\texttt{edge-tts}) and a plain-English explanation module for non-legal audiences.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Speech Recognition and Neural Diarization}
Transformer-based speech recognition models such as OpenAI's Whisper \cite{radford2023whisper} have established state-of-the-art benchmarks in open-vocabulary ASR. Quantized implementations like \texttt{faster-whisper} utilize CTranslate2 to optimize GPU VRAM usage during long-form batch processing. For speaker turn identification, PyAnnote Audio 3.1 \cite{bredin2023pyannote} employs SincNet feature extraction and HNSW clustering, providing robust boundary separation in conversational settings.

\subsection{Dense Passage Retrieval and Spoken RAG}
Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) \cite{karpukhin2020dpr} and RAG architectures \cite{lewis2020rag} replace traditional BM25 sparse retrieval with dense bi-encoder embeddings. Pre-trained transformer encoders such as BGE-Large \cite{baai2023bge} map passages into high-dimensional metric space. While Spoken RAG extends dense retrieval to speech transcripts, domain-specific legal applications require hybrid filtering to prevent statutory ambiguity.


\section{Phase 1: Dataset Curation and Audio Processing Pipeline}

The Phase 1 pipeline converts raw, unscripted legal video proceedings into structured dialogue chunks with speaker provenance and timestamp metadata.

\subsection{Audio Signal Conditioning}
Raw video files (\texttt{.mp4}) are ingested via \texttt{ffmpeg} and converted into standardized 16\,kHz, 16-bit mono PCM WAV audio signals. This eliminates channel imbalance and acoustic artifacts before downstream neural processing.

\subsection{PyAnnote 3.1 Neural Speaker Diarization}
To address speaker boundary drift in multi-speaker arguments, we deploy \texttt{pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1}. The model segments audio tracks into distinct speaker intervals ($\text{SPEAKER\_00}, \text{SPEAKER\_01}, \dots$), generating precise timestamped turn intervals $(t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}})$.

\subsection{Fault-Tolerant ASR Transcription}
Audio segments are processed using \texttt{faster-whisper} (\texttt{small} model, \texttt{int8} quantization). To prevent loss during long batch executions, a custom state manager persists per-segment transcriptions into \texttt{.partial.json} files, enabling seamless fault-tolerant resumption without re-computing prior segments.

\subsection{Dialogue Sliding-Window Chunking}
Transcripts are aggregated into sliding-window dialogue chunks of 150--300 words. Each chunk retains metadata fields including \texttt{source\_type}, \texttt{case\_name}, \texttt{track}, \texttt{video\_id}, \texttt{start\_time}, \texttt{end\_time}, and \texttt{speakers}.


\section{Phase 2: Dense Vector RAG and Synthesis Engine}

\subsection{BGE-Large Vector Indexing}
Statutory provisions (Representation of the People Act 1951, Code of Criminal Procedure, Constitution of India) and dialogue transcripts are embedded using \texttt{BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5} (1024 dimensions) and stored in ChromaDB using HNSW distance indexing (\texttt{legal\_speech\_rag\_bge}).

\subsection{Canonical Act Mapping and Exact Metadata Section Boosting}
To resolve vector space collisions, incoming user queries are parsed by a query analyzer. Explicit statutory targets (e.g., ``Section 9A'', ``Article 14'') trigger exact metadata section filtering:
\begin{equation}
\text{Score}_{\text{final}}(c) = 
\begin{cases} 
0.9500 & \text{if } c.\text{section\_id} = q.\text{target\_section} \land c.\text{act\_name} \in q.\text{acts} \\
1.0 - d_{\text{cosine}}(q, c) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This guarantees $100\%$ precision for explicit section references while eliminating cross-act contamination.

\subsection{Plain-English Explanation Generator}
To assist non-legal users, a specialized LLM module transforms complex statutory answers into plain-English explanations formatted under three strict sections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{\#\#\# In Simple Words}: Plain-English breakdown (2--4 paragraphs).
    \item \texttt{\#\#\# Simple Example}: Practical scenario illustrating the rule.
    \item \texttt{\#\#\# Important Point}: One sentence highlighting key statutory limitations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Neural Speech Synthesis}
Grounding outputs are converted into high-fidelity audible explanations using \texttt{edge-tts} with neural Indian English voices (\texttt{en-IN-NeerjaNeural} female and \texttt{en-IN-PrabhatNeural} male).

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
We presented a complete framework for legal speech dataset curation and Spoken RAG QA. By indexing 84.98 hours of multi-speaker legal audio into a 4,260-chunk BGE-Large vector store with metadata section boosting, our system eliminates cross-act section collisions, achieves 100.0\% Context Precision, and generates both neural voice output and layman-friendly explanations. Future work includes fine-tuning Whisper ASR on specialized Indian legal vocabulary and expanding real-time web streaming capabilities.
